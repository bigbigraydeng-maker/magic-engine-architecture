## FULL DRAFT

### Selling Your Home in Kohimarama: 2026 Pricing, Timing & Marketing Guide

If you're selling in Kohimarama in 2026, here's the short version: the suburb is currently pricing slightly above neighbouring Mission Bay and selling a little faster, but Kohimarama's buyer pool responds to different things — character-home charm and land value, not just harbour views — so a pricing and marketing approach copied from a generic Auckland suburb guide will undersell what makes your specific property work. Here's how pricing, timing and marketing actually play out in Kohimarama right now, from an agent who works this suburb alongside Mission Bay, St Heliers and Glendowie every week.

#### Kohimarama's 2026 price picture

Kohimarama's average house value currently sits at **$2,046,400**, down 2.7% from where it was two years ago (source: Opes Partners, using LINZ Data Service records, retrieved August 2026). For context, that puts Kohimarama a little above neighbouring Mission Bay's current average of $1,978,250 — roughly 3.5% higher on this measure. The two suburbs are close geographically but price slightly differently, and Kohimarama's mix of character villas alongside newer builds is part of why.

The pace of sales is also a touch faster in Kohimarama than Mission Bay right now: the median time to sell is **40 days**, against Mission Bay's 44. Over the trailing 12 months, **75 properties** have sold in the suburb, with around 10 new listings coming to market each month.

Those property-type breakdowns matter more in Kohimarama than in a more architecturally uniform suburb — a renovated character villa and a new-build apartment a street apart can price very differently, and a single suburb-wide average doesn't tell you where your specific property sits.

#### Best time of year to list in Kohimarama

Auckland's Eastern Bays suburbs typically see a seasonal pattern in listing volume and buyer activity, and Kohimarama is no exception — though its pattern doesn't always track Mission Bay's exactly, given the different buyer profile each suburb attracts. `[NEEDS DATA: REINZ Auckland Regional Report — Kohimarama/Eastern Bays seasonal listing volume by month, 2025–2026]`

Rather than defaulting to a generic "spring is best" rule of thumb, I plan listing timing around the current seasonal data plus each vendor's own circumstances — a character villa that photographs beautifully in a well-established garden often benefits from a different listing window than a new-build apartment with year-round street appeal.

#### What Kohimarama buyers actually value

Kohimarama's buyer pool responds to a distinct set of factors:

- **Beach proximity and Tamaki Drive access** — the suburb's waterfront edge and cycling/running route along Tamaki Drive is a genuine lifestyle draw, not just a marketing line.
- **School zone** — as in Mission Bay, zoned schools shape buyer demand and willingness to pay.
- **Character-home stock** — Kohimarama has a meaningfully higher proportion of character villas and bungalows than some neighbouring suburbs, and there's a buyer segment specifically seeking that heritage character, distinct from buyers purely chasing land value or a rebuild opportunity.

Understanding which of these matters most for your specific property — and to which buyer segment — shapes both the appraisal and the marketing plan.

#### Pricing strategy: comparable sales, not a generic Auckland estimate

A desktop valuation tool applies a broad Auckland-wide (or at best suburb-wide) model. It can't tell the difference between a character villa on a quiet street and a modern townhouse on a busier road, and in Kohimarama specifically, that gap matters more than in more architecturally uniform suburbs. A proper appraisal weighs:

- Recent comparable sales of a similar property type, not suburb-wide averages
- Land size and any rebuild or renovation potential
- Street-level factors (proximity to the beach and Tamaki Drive, school zone boundaries)
- Condition and presentation relative to comparable character-home sales specifically, where relevant

#### Marketing your Kohimarama home

Kohimarama's more character-home-heavy buyer pool means the marketing mix should shift from a straight Mission Bay-style campaign. Photography and copy that lean into heritage features — original joinery, established gardens, period details — tend to resonate with the buyer segment specifically seeking character stock, alongside the same fundamentals that apply suburb-wide: professional photography and video, proper placement across the major property portals, and direct access to Auckland's Chinese-buyer pool through native-language conversations rather than a translator relaying a diluted version of the listing.

As a trilingual agent (English, Mandarin, Shanghainese and Cantonese) who's worked the Mission Bay–Kohimarama–St Heliers–Glendowie corridor for nine years, I tailor the marketing angle to what's actually driving interest in each specific property, rather than running one campaign template across every suburb.

#### Common vendor mistakes in Kohimarama

The two mistakes I see most often in this suburb:

**Overpricing against the wrong comparables.** Using a nearby but architecturally different sale (a new-build price applied to a character villa, or vice versa) as your pricing anchor sets an unrealistic expectation from day one — and an overpriced character home tends to sit noticeably longer than one priced against genuinely comparable sales.

**Underinvesting in kerb presentation.** In a character-home suburb, first impressions from the street carry disproportionate weight — buyers touring Kohimarama are often specifically drawn by street-level charm, and a villa that looks tired from the footpath loses interest before a buyer even steps inside, regardless of how the interior presents.
